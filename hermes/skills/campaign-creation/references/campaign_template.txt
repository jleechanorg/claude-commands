## Part 1: Systemic Sub-Templates
### Sub-Template A: Standard Character Architecture
Whenever this prompt instructs you to generate or detail any character (including the Protagonist, Retinue Members, Parents, Siblings, or Faction Leaders), you must explicitly execute their background using this exact six-tier architectural profile:
 * I. Core Identity: Name, Archetype/Role, Social Standing/Starting Title, Moral Framework/Alignment, and MBTI Type.
 * II. Psychology: Core Motivation (The "Why"), Greatest Fear (rooted in specific formative trauma), and a variable range of 3 to 5 distinct Temperament Traits.
 * III. Behavior and Speech: Physical manifestations of stress/subconscious ticks, explicit Speech Patterns (tone, cadence, vocabulary preferences, pacing), and Public Reputation.
 * IV. Complete Backstory:
   * The Defining Moment: The singular, catalytic event that irrevocably forged their current identity, detailing the precise sequence of action, choice, and perceived failure.
   * Relevant History: Chronological history of their upbringing, early environment, specialized training, and the explicit path to their starting position.
   * Deep Secrets: Hidden past actions, sins, or internal layers of guilt completely unknown to the public or other characters.
 * V. Persona vs. Repressed Interior: The outward-facing mask (how they intentionally manipulate their environment) vs. how they internally analyze and critique their own performance when alone.
 * VI. Unconscious Beliefs: A range of 2 to 4 deep-seated, absolute statements they subconsciously believe about themselves or the universe (e.g., "I am the cause of all suffering," "Power is the only objective truth").
### Sub-Template B: Faction Structural Profile
Whenever formatting a major faction or organization, apply this structural profile:
 * I. Nomenclature and Heraldry: Official Name, Common Alias, and Sensory Visual Signature (Colors, Sigils, Architecture, Uniforms).
 * II. Infrastructure and Domain: Primary Headquarters (Name, Location, Vibe), Estimated Asset/Manpower Bracket (expressed as a broad scale), and Primary Resource Engine (How they fund their operations).
 * III. Leadership Dossier: Apply Sub-Template A in full to the current leader of the organization.
 * IV. Internal Operational Culture: The unwritten codes of conduct, internal power struggles, and what happens to members who fail or betray the organization.
 * V. The Hook: The precise structural vulnerability, historical scandal, or personal trauma of the leader that the protagonist can actively exploit to dismantle or control the faction.
### Sub-Template C: Tactical Masterwork / Relic Framework
Whenever designing magical or masterwork items for the Panoply or Loot Tables, apply this framework:
 * I. Item Name and Classification: True Name, Legendary Titles, and Base Item Type (e.g., Plate Armor, Spellcasting Focus).
 * II. Aesthetic and Material: Exact description of its physical appearance, components, weight, age, and tactile feel.
 * III. Mythic Origin: The lore history of who forged it, the historical battles or betrayals it was present for, and how it was acquired.
 * IV. System Metrics (5e Integration):
   * Passive Property: A continuous narrative, sensory, or exploratory boon.
   * Active Tactical Feature: A high-impact ability with specific action economy cost, variable daily uses or recharge dice rolls, and scaling Difficulty Class (DC) calculations.
   * Narrative Side Effect / Behavioral Urge: A thematic drawback or psychological pull imposed on the attuner.

 * Inner Monologue: A variable selection of 3 to 5 raw sample thoughts showing their internal worldview and reactions to severe stress.
### Section 3: Character Class (The Custom Build)

   * Tier 1 (Levels 1-5): Renamed core features + 1 Custom Class Ability.

   * Tier 3 (Levels 11-16): Renamed core features + 1 High-Impact Offensive Capstone.
   * Tier 4 (Levels 17-20): The Transformation / Avatar State mechanic representing peak mortality, specifying trigger actions and duration.
   * Tier 5 (Levels 21-30): Named Epic Boons, Attribute cap increases (allowing scores up to a maximum range of 30), and reality-bending capabilities.
### Section 4: Assets and The Retinue
 * Starting Status: Starting Rank, Noble Title, or Institutional Position matching their high station.
 * Resources: Broad starting wealth ranges, specific location descriptions and defenses for a randomized bracket of secure safehouses, and actionable political leverage or blackmail material against secondary characters.
 * The Panoply: A variable assortment of 3 to 5 Masterwork or Magical items matching their social station. Execute each item using Sub-Template C (Tactical Masterwork / Relic Framework).
 * The Retinue: A small, specialized cohort of 3 to 4 distinct NPC subordinates. For each retinue member, apply Sub-Template A in full, and explicitly append their Loyalty Profile (the psychological, financial, or transactional reason they serve the protagonist instead of betraying them).
### Section 5: Family Dynamics
 * The Parents: Two powerful figures (typically Level 12 to 20+) who heavily influence the protagonist's starting social standing. For each parent, apply Sub-Template A in full. Randomly determine their underlying stance toward the protagonist by selecting from a fluid spectrum: a Protective/Nurturing Guide, a Demanding/Indifferent Superior, or a Covert Adversary, detailing how this dynamically impacts the player's faction access.
 * The Siblings: Generate exactly two older brothers and two older sisters. For each individual sibling, apply Sub-Template A in full. Explicitly randomize their core relationship dynamic with the protagonist by rolling or choosing from a variable matrix of structural roles, mapping out their personal goals:
   1. Direct Ally (a loyal, trustworthy confidant who shares resources),
   2. Overprotective Guardian (loyal but highly restrictive of player actions),
   3. Indifferent Bystander (pursuing their own independent agenda away from the main plot),
   4. Unwitting Pawn (an easily manipulated asset whose resources can be drained),
   5. Hostile Rival (an active threat to safety, status, or inheritance).
### Section 6: Factions (The Game Board)
 * Ruling Factions / Major Houses: Exactly 10 Major Houses, Corporations, or Clans. For each faction, apply Sub-Template B (Faction Structural Profile) in full, ensuring exhaustive detail.
 * Friendly Factions: Exactly 10 Groups (Armies, Cults, Guilds) the protagonist can leverage as operational tools. Provide their Name, Operational Function, Leader's Identity, and the precise Leverage Point or Contract that keeps them aligned.
 * Antagonistic Factions: Exactly 10 Threats. Focus purely on Humanoid or Structural threats (Assassins, Rival Empires, Inquisitions, Corporate Strike Teams) rather than mindless monsters. For each, describe their Primary Objective and their Escalation Method (how they react when the protagonist thwarts them).
### Section 7: World Lore
 * The Timeline: A detailed list of major historical events leading up to the campaign start, identifying the exact Divergence Point or unique ancient occurrence that shapes the current era.
 * The Mythos: The metaphysical reality of the world, detailing the pantheon of Gods, the true nature of the Magic/Technology systems, and active Ancient Pacts or cosmological constraints.
 * Current Situation: The immediate crisis (War, Succession Crisis, Plague, Looming Collapse) and the active conflict vector forcing immediate action.
 * Story Arcs: 3 potential sequential phases (Early, Mid, Late Game) outlining escalating stakes, regional milestones, and potential systemic endings while preserving complete player choice.
### Section 8: Gazetteer and Mechanics
 * Locations: A varied spread of 4 to 6 Key Stages (e.g., The Palace, The War Camp, The Slums, The Spire). Include The Vibe, a range of 3 to 5 Key Sub-locations within each stage, and a concrete Physical or Social Hazard (complete with variable 5e mechanical DC brackets and consequences) for each stage.
 * Custom Mechanics: Design exactly 2 unique gameplay sub-systems to support the narrative theme. Provide full rules, trackers, and modifiers for these systems. Choose from:
   * Mass Combat / Unit Rules: How the player commands armies or cohorts using modified 5e metrics.
   * Social Reputation Tracker: A systemic tracker (e.g., Dignitas, Infamy, Leverage Bars) that shifts faction behaviors based on public deeds.
   * Resource Management Ledger: Rules for tracking structural wealth, supply lines, or institutional influence.
 * Loot Table: A clean table of 8 to 12 unique non-standard "Relics" or "Favors" appropriate for the setting. Do not write generic +1 items; focus purely on systemic, narrative, or mechanical power shifts.
### Section 9: Starting Scene
 * Setting: Evocative, atmospheric description of the opening location utilizing sensory details (sights, sounds, smells, ambient lighting).
 * The Hook: The immediate context, crisis, or operational opportunity framing the protagonist right now.
 * The Action: The first decision point. Provide an immediate A/B/C choice that bypasses tests of authority and allows the player to act, dominate, build, or demonstrate power right away, establishing their high position in the world.
